People, place & living traditions

Kerala's culture is lived, not staged.

There is no single story that speaks for everyone in Kerala. Traditions change across regions, communities and generations. This is a starting point for learning with curiosity, and for meeting people on their own terms.

Stories in movement, music & ritual

Performance has many forms.

Kerala's performance traditions are not interchangeable shows: each has its own history, artists, setting and relationship with the community.

01 / Stage & story

Kathakali

A highly stylised dance-drama combining gesture, music, movement and elaborate costume and make-up. Performances can draw on stories from Indian epics. If you attend, look for an established venue or troupe, check whether an introduction is offered, and let the performance speak for itself.

02 / Northern Kerala

Theyyam

A family of ritual performance traditions associated especially with parts of northern Kerala. Many performances take place in sacred community settings and are connected to local worship. Visitors should follow the hosts' guidance; attendance, photography and access are not automatically open to the public.

These are just two entry points. Kerala has a wide range of classical, folk, ritual and contemporary arts, with practices and names that vary by place.

Dates and venues can change. Confirm with the organisers or venue before travelling, and do not treat a community ceremony as a guaranteed tourist attraction.

A calendar shaped by communities

Festivals are more than dates.

Onam is widely celebrated in Kerala, but the way it is observed differs between families and communities. Local temple festivals and other religious observances have their own meanings, customs and access expectations.

Seasonal celebration

Onam

Homes and public spaces may mark the season with pookkalam (flower arrangements), shared meals, games and local events. What happens, and when, varies by place and year; look to local organisers for current details rather than assuming a set visitor programme.

Local, not one-size-fits-all

Temple & community festivals

Processions, music and performances may be part of local observances, but customs are specific to their setting. Check whether visitors are welcome, follow signs and host instructions, and give processions and worshippers space.

Thrissur · Temple festival

Thrissur Pooram

A major festival celebrated in Thrissur, with processions and ceremonial displays. The event is crowded and rooted in local religious traditions; check current dates, visitor arrangements and local guidance before attending, and follow instructions at the site.

A table with many influences

Taste the regional differences.

Kerala food is not one menu. Ingredients, preparation and everyday dishes vary across the coast and highlands, between districts, and among the state's many communities.

Rice, coconut & more

Ask what is local

Meals may include rice, coconut, spices, vegetables, fish or meat, but combinations and recipes are local. Appam, puttu, fish curry and sadya are familiar examples, not a complete picture of what people eat.

Every kitchen is different

Eat with curiosity

Ask what is seasonal and how a dish is prepared. If you have allergies or dietary needs, speak directly with the restaurant about ingredients and cross-contact; don't assume a dish is suitable from its name alone.

Travel with care

Be a thoughtful guest.

A few small choices can make visits more considerate. When unsure, ask the people who host a place or event and accept their answer.

  • Ask before photographing people, religious spaces, ceremonies or performances; respect a no-photo rule.
  • Check dress and visitor guidance for places of worship, and follow local instructions about where visitors may go.
  • Arrive as an observer, not an entitled audience. Some traditions are sacred or community-only, not performances for visitors.
  • Support local artists and businesses fairly, and ask before entering private, working or restricted areas.
